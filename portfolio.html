Undergraduate Researcher — University of Houston

Thermal modelling for a research instrument under development in a university lab. My piece of the work is the heat problem: how much heat a key powered component makes, where that heat gets stuck on its way out, and how long the system can run before temperature becomes the limit.

  • Built a MATLAB thermal model of the component board — a junction-to-ambient resistance network through package, board, thermal interface material, and heatsink. The result that mattered: board conduction dominates the thermal budget — and the standard fix on its own can’t close the gap.
  • Followed it up in COMSOL Multiphysics 6.4 with transient thermal runs of the real-size board (3×9 in). At full power, an aluminum-core MCPCB held the component at ~89 °C after 60 seconds, with the curve flattening toward a ~95–100 °C plateau — well under the 125 °C limit — and it won’t run at max power in practice, so there’s more margin than that. At real size the top copper spreads heat well enough that the board’s dielectric layer stops being the bottleneck the small test-piece numbers suggested; most of the board stays near ambient, with a small hot spot at the component.
  • Recommendation from the modelling: aluminum-core MCPCB, no heatsink needed — cheaper than a copper substrate, and better than FR4 with a heatsink, since FR4’s bottleneck is the board itself and a heatsink can’t fix that.
  • Prep work was a two-part analog skill-builder: a rheostat dimmer for no-PWM brightness control, then a PWM test — blink, PWM dimmer, and sinusoidal-brightness sketches.
